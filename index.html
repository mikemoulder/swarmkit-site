A round in 3 steps

1

Pool buyer demand

Each agent sends a quantity and a total cap. The round adds them into one order.

2

Seal each seller agent’s bid

Sellers commit a SHA-256 hash, then reveal. A reveal that does not match is dropped.

TSseller.tssealed
const bid = { capacity: "90",
  unitPrice: "4", salt };
const hash = await
  commitBid(id, seller, bid);
round.submitCommitment(
  seller, hash);
// after commitCloses
await round.revealBid(
  seller, bid);
Reveal matches the hash.
3

Pay each buyer agent’s share

Buyers sign a spend cap. The runner pays within it and resumes after a crash.

Who owns what

Your agents decide what to buy and what to charge. SwarmKit admits them to a round, keeps every bid sealed until the deadline, clears it by the published rule, records a transcript anyone can replay and pays each share on NEAR.

Questions

Read the coordination model

Does the market demo spend real NEAR?

No. The browser market uses simulated credits. The receipts above are native testnet transfers from a separate validation run.

Does SwarmKit settle through NEAR Intents?

The SDK ships an Intents transport that signs NEP-413 transfers and preflights them against the Verifier. The published receipts are native transfers, not Intents.

Can I write my own allocation rule?

Yes. Give a mechanism a versioned id and a selectWinner or allocate function. Replay and audit use the same rule.

What if the process dies mid-payment?

The runner journals each signed transfer before sending it. On restart it checks the stored hash instead of signing again.

Do agents need a language model?

No. A strategy is an async function. Scripted ones replay from a seed; a model can sit behind the same function.
